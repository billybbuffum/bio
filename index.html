Bio 101: Life, DNA & the Human Body

An interactive introduction to biology. Start with atoms and water, work up through cells, energy, DNA and genetics, and finish with how your own body works. Every week has a reading, a set of key-term flashcards, hands-on virtual labs, a practical you can do at home, and a quiz.

How each week works

  1. Read
    A 45–70 minute lesson with diagrams, "check yourself" questions and human-body connections.
  2. Review key terms
    Flip through flashcards until you know every term.
  3. Virtual lab
    Run simulations, collect data, and answer worksheet questions.
  4. Practical
    A real experiment with household materials. Record data and analyze it.
  5. Quiz
    Score 70%+ to pass. Retake as often as you like, with explanations for every answer.

Plan on roughly 4–6 hours per week. Everything you type (notes, data, answers) is saved automatically in this browser. Back it up from My Progress.

Practical shopping list

Everything is cheap and easy to find. Most of it is probably already in your kitchen.

  • Produce: red cabbage (W1), potatoes (W2, W3), strawberries (W5), optional fresh and canned pineapple (W2)
  • Pantry: salt, sugar, baking soda, vinegar, lemon juice, active dry yeast (W4), dry beans, rice and pasta (W8), gelatin (optional)
  • Pharmacy: 3% hydrogen peroxide (W2), 70–91% rubbing alcohol (W5)
  • Household: dish soap, clear cups, zip-top bags, coffee filters, balloons, small plastic bottles, string, ruler, kitchen scale, coins, paper clips, utensils (spoon, fork, tweezers, clothespin), timer
Safety firstEach practical lists its own safety notes. Have an adult help with boiling water and knives, never taste lab materials, and never mix bleach with other chemicals.

About this course

Bio 101 follows the topics of a typical first-semester college or advanced high-school biology course, with extra emphasis on DNA and human biology. All simulations run in your browser with no accounts and no tracking. The free OpenStax Biology 2e textbook is linked each week as optional companion reading, and Anatomy & Physiology 2e is great if the human-body weeks leave you wanting more.

Health-related content is educational and is not medical advice.
